Step 1 of 7

Review the System Brief

Crestview State University, a fictional public university in the United States, piloted PathAlert in one college for a semester. PathAlert is an AI-driven advising system that analyzes student records and behavioral data to identify students at risk of dropping out.

How it works

  • Uses machine learning to give each student a risk score from 0 to 100.
  • Students above a set threshold are labeled "high risk."
  • Flagged students receive automated text messages about deadlines and support.
  • Leadership wants to use the score in scholarship renewal decisions.

Pilot results

  • More students received timely reminders about registration and financial aid.
  • Advisors reported faster identification of students needing support.
  • Leadership sees strong efficiency gains and wants to expand.

Concerns raised in an internal review

  • Students from certain ZIP codes are flagged far more often than others.
  • Students were never told that AI was monitoring their data.
  • Flags arrive without any explanation of why a student was labeled.
  • No independent audit of the model's performance has taken place.
  • Some advisors act on scores without questioning them.

Your role

Your committee must refine PathAlert, not replace it. In the next steps, you will review its data inputs, decide how scores are used, strengthen transparency, and design a governance structure. The live System Readout panel shows how each decision changes the system.

Step 2 of 7 · Bias and data governance

Audit the Data Inputs

AI models are shaped by their training data and inputs. If those inputs are flawed, incomplete, or biased, the outputs will be too. The vendor switched on every available input. Turn inputs on or off and watch how labeling accuracy, the flag gap between student groups, and privacy protection change.
